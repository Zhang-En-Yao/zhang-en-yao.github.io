# Madrid

Lodging: Ronda de Atocha 28

### Royal Palace & the Old Quarter

The Habsburg-era old town centered on the Royal Palace — churches, plazas, and the market all within walking distance.

| Place | Type | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palacio Real de Madrid | Sight | 10/24 | The Spanish royal family's official palace, one of the largest in Europe still standing. Houses royal furniture, armor, and tapestries; now used mainly for state ceremonies. |
| Catedral de la Almudena | Sight | 10/24 | Madrid's cathedral, right beside the palace. Blends Neo-Gothic and Neoclassical styles; construction began in the 19th century but it wasn't consecrated until 1993, by Pope John Paul II. |
| Plaza de Oriente | Plaza | 10/24 | Directly in front of the palace. An equestrian statue of Philip IV stands at its center, ringed by statues of past Spanish monarchs. |
| Plaza Mayor | Plaza | 10/24 | Madrid's most iconic Baroque plaza, completed in the 17th century. Has hosted coronations, bullfights, and Inquisition trials. |
| Mercado de San Miguel | Sight | 10/24 | A steel-and-glass market hall from 1916, now a food market — tapas of every kind, plus jamón ibérico. |
| La Latina | District | 10/25 | One of Madrid's oldest neighborhoods, known for the Sunday flea market El Rastro and its dense cluster of tapas bars. |
| Real Basílica de San Francisco el Grande | Church | 10/25 | Home to one of Spain's largest domes; holds early paintings by Goya. |

### Sarria

Lodging: Pensión-Albergue Puente Ribeira (Rua do Peregrino, 23, 27600 Sarria, Spain)

A small Galician town and the Camino Francés's most popular starting point — just over 100 km from Santiago, meeting the minimum distance for the pilgrim's certificate.

### Portomarín

Lodging: Pension Perez (Praza Aviacion Espanola 2, 27170 Portomarín, Spain)

A town rebuilt after its original site was flooded by a reservoir; the old town now lies underwater. The Church of San Juan was dismantled stone by stone and reassembled at the new site.

### Gonzar

Lodging: Hosteria de Gonzar (3 Gonzar, 27188 Gonzar, Spain)

A typical small Galician village on the Camino Francés, mostly a cluster of small inns for pilgrims to rest partway through the day.

### Palas de Rei

Lodging: Pension Pardellas (Rua do Mercado, nº 13, 27200 Palas de Rei, Spain)

An inland Galician town, an important waypoint on the pilgrim route since the Middle Ages; its name means "the king's palace."

### Melide

Lodging: Pensión Pereiro (Calle Progreso 43, 15800 Melide, Spain)

A Galician town famous for pulpo à feira (Galician-style octopus), and the point where the Camino Francés meets the Camino del Norte.

### Arzúa

Lodging: Casa do Cabo (Rúa Alcalde Juan Vidal, n13-15, 15810 Arzúa, Spain)

Known for its namesake cheese, Queso de Arzúa — the third-to-last major stop before reaching Santiago.

### O Pedrouzo

Lodging: Pensión A Solaina 3 (Rúa Minas Numero 2, Portal 1, 2 A, 15821 O Pedrouzo, Spain)

A town about 20 km from Santiago — most pilgrims' last night's stop before reaching the end.

### Santiago de Compostela

Lodging: Parador de Santiago de Compostela (Praza do Obradoiro, 1, 15705 Santiago de Compostela, Spain)

The first night at the end of the road: a state-run Parador beside the cathedral square, converted from a 15th-century pilgrims' hospital.

### Santiago de Compostela

Lodging: PR Paz de Agra (Calderería, 37, 15704 Santiago de Compostela, Spain)

The endpoint of the Camino de Santiago. Its cathedral is said to hold the relics of the Apostle James, making it one of Catholicism's three great pilgrimage sites — a chance to keep exploring the old town's lanes.

# Barcelona

Lodging: Rooms Borrells, Carrer del Comte Borrell, 9, 08015 Barcelona, Spain

The last and longest-running stop of the trip in historical terms — from the Roman colony of Barcino, through the medieval Crown of Aragon's Mediterranean trading capital, to the loss of self-government after the 1714 siege, the 19th-century industrialization and Catalan cultural revival (the Renaixença), Modernisme and Gaudí, the labor movement and the Spanish Civil War, the Franco years, and on to democratization and the 1992 Olympics reopening the waterfront.
